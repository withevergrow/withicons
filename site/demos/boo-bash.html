🗓️ Run of show

Three hours of pure silly

Everything below happens in the backyard and around Maple Court. Times are a guide, but the parade starts at 3:00 sharp because Uncle Dayo's kazoo band has a very tight schedule.

  1. 🕑

    2:00 pm

    Doors open

    Check in at the side gate, grab a name tag and your costume number for the contest. Grown-ups who are dropping off sign the sheet here.

  2. 🕑

    2:15 pm

    Craft stations open

    Pumpkin painting, the slime lab, bat masks and the mummy wrap race. Smocks are on the hooks. Wander and try everything.

  3. 🕒

    2:50 pm

    Line up, little monsters

    Wash paint off hands (mostly), find your costume number and line up by the hay bales. Grown-ups, get your cameras out.

  4. 🕒

    3:00 pm The big one

    Costume parade

    Once around the pumpkin patch and down the driveway, led by Uncle Dayo's kazoo band. Roar, flap, wobble or moo. Everybody gets a round of applause.

  5. 🕒

    3:20 pm

    Trick-or-treat trail

    Six friendly stops around Maple Court with a stamp card. Takes about 35 minutes, and ages 3 to 5 walk it with a grown-up.

  6. 🕓

    4:00 pm

    Snack time

    Ghost cupcakes, mummy dogs and swamp punch on the long table. Every label shows what is in it, and nothing contains nuts.

  7. 🕓

    4:20 pm

    Ballots close

    Last chance to drop your costume ballot in the pumpkin box. Judges Dayo, Tamsin and Pip count in secret.

  8. 🕟

    4:30 pm

    Winners and birthday song

    Four trophies, one very loud rendition of Happy Birthday and a pumpkin cupcake for Pip with a single candle.

  9. 🕔

    5:00 pm

    Home time

    Goody bags at the gate, pick-up with your code word, and a sleepy wave goodbye. Drop-off kids must be collected by 5:00.

              ❓ Good questions

              Before you ask

              Is it really nut-free?▾

              Yes. Peanuts and tree nuts are not allowed anywhere at the party, the snack table is checked by two adults, and the cupcake maker runs a nut-free kitchen. Sesame, milk, egg, wheat and soy do appear, so every dish is labelled with exactly what is in it.

              What if it rains?▾

              We move to the garage and the big tent and run the trail as a hallway hunt with the same stamps. We text everyone on Saturday by 11 am. A light drizzle does not cancel a pumpkin patch.

              Do grown-ups have to dress up?▾

              No, but they are very welcome to. Costumes are the dress code for kids, and anything silly counts: a cardboard rocket, a banana, a very small pumpkin. Please skip masks that cover the eyes.

              Is anything scary?▾

              No gore, no jump scares, no real-looking weapons. The scariest thing on the trail is a rubber spider named Gerald, and he is apologetic about it. The spook-o-meter on each stop tells you exactly how mild it is.

              Can younger siblings come?▾

              Yes, and they are free. Under-3s stay with a grown-up the whole time. There is a toddler corner with soft blocks and a quiet tent for naps.

              Should we bring a present for Pip?▾

              No presents, please. If you would like to bring something, bring a favourite picture book for the Little Library box by the gate. Pip will read it at bedtime for a year.
