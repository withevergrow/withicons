👩‍🍳 The method

How a cookie swap works

Three steps and a little arithmetic. Everybody bakes one kind, everybody leaves with twelve.

  1. 1 👩‍🍳

    Bake 3½ dozen of one kind

    Claim your cookie on the sign-up sheet first, so we get twelve different kinds and not five gingerbreads. Bake 42: 36 for the swap, 6 for the tasting table. Let them cool all the way.

    Nell’s rule: cool means cool.

    →
  2. 2 📦

    Bring them boxed, with 12 recipe cards

    Layer them in a lidded tin or box with parchment between. Write your recipe on twelve cards (index cards, napkins, whatever you have) and flag nuts or gluten on the lid. Cookies go on the sorting table by 4:30.

    Messy handwriting is part of the charm.

    →
  3. 3 🔄

    Walk the line, fill your box

    At 5:45 we all walk the long table together. Take three of every kind into your own tin (twelve kinds, thirty-six cookies), grab the recipe cards, then go back for cocoa.

    Sampling is allowed. The whole row is not.

⏰ Oven schedule

Four hours, start to finish

Doors at four. The swap itself happens at 5:45 sharp, because the cocoa waits for no one.

  1. 🕓
    4:00 pm

    Doors and cocoa

    Sweaters on, name tags on, cocoa bar open. The kids’ table is stocked with felt-tip pens.

  2. 🕟
    4:30 pm

    Cookies to the sorting table

    Open your tin, set out your recipe cards and your nut or gluten flag. Abe counts. Twice.

  3. 🕔
    5:00 pm

    The decorating table

    Nell’s pre-baked sugar cookies, a rainbow of icing bags and one very serious jar of edible glitter.

  4. 🕠
    5:45 pm · the main event

    The Swap

    Walk the line, take three of every kind. Please queue clockwise; Nell has opinions.

  5. 🕡
    6:30 pm

    Sweater parade and vote

    Everyone gets a turn on the hallway runway. Ballots close at the bell.

  6. 🕖
    7:00 pm

    Winners, a toast, and the leftovers

    Prizes, a toast (cocoa or otherwise), then every leftover cookie is boxed for the Pantry.

  7. 🕗
    8:00 pm

    Last call

    Doors close. Please take your tin home; a name label on the lid helps us hand it back.

❓ Good to know

Questions, answered

Do I have to bake?▾

No. Bakers take home the full thirty-six, but tasters are very welcome: you’ll eat from the table, decorate, vote and pour cocoa. Choose “Taste and cheer” on the RSVP and, if you like, tell us which cocoa topping you’ll bring.

Can I bring store-bought cookies?▾

The swap itself is homemade only, because that is the whole point. A bakery box for the tasting table is always welcome, though.

What if the kind I want is already claimed?▾

Each kind can only be claimed once, first come first served. Check the sign-up sheet again on Sunday: bakers do cancel, and a cup frees up the moment they release it.

How do you handle allergies?▾

Every kind is flagged on the sheet and every tin on the table wears a flag card (nuts, gluten-free, vegan). Each kind has its own tongs, and nut cookies live on a separate board by the window. Please mention severe allergies in your RSVP and we’ll talk it through.

Are kids welcome?▾

Very. They count toward your party of four. The kids’ table has felt-tips from 4:00 and icing from 5:00, and there is a quiet room upstairs with a blanket fort.

I don’t own an ugly sweater.▾

Nobody does until December. Abe keeps a basket of fourteen thrift-shop knits by the door: borrow one, or wear the most festive thing you own. A reindeer headband counts.

Where do I park, and is it step-free?▾

Use the Birch Lane lot (two minutes on foot) or take Route 14 to Spruce & 9th. The side gate on Birch Lane is step-free, and a rolling cart is waiting there for your tins.

What happens to the leftover cookies?▾

At 7:30 they are boxed in kraft paper and delivered to the Alder Falls Community Pantry the same evening. Last year that was 214 cookies in 17 boxes.

Can I change my mind after I RSVP?▾

Yes, until Friday, December 4. Edit your RSVP and switch to “Taste and cheer”, and your cup goes straight back on the sign-up sheet for someone else.

See you on the twelfth

Warm kitchen, cold snow, twelve kinds of cookie.

We started this swap in 2019 with six bakers and one very full oven. It has grown, but the rule hasn’t: bring something you’re proud of, leave with something you’ve never tried.
