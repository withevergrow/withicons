🕓 The day, hour by hour

Six hours of light, one Saturday

Everything on the main lawn runs to this clock, and the stalls stay open all evening. The board follows your device's time; pick a demo time to see how the day looks at any hour.

    Gates and stalls open

    Come in through Gate A on Marigold Street. Councillor Meera Joshi lights the first diya at 4:15, Food Lane fires up and the info tent hands out free festival maps.

    📍 Gate A and Food Lane🎟️ Free entry

    🎨

    Rangoli contest: the plaza becomes a canvas

    Thirty-six teams colour their plots with powder, petals and lamps while the judges stroll. Spectators are welcome to watch, ask questions and cheer. Sign-up is open until the plots run out.

    📍 Rangoli Ground⏳ 90 minutes

  3. 6:30pm
    🏆

    Judging, winners and a garba warm-up

    Prizes are announced from the stage, then a 15-minute lesson teaches the three steps you need for the circle. Absolute beginners and two left feet are the whole point.

    📍 Main stage👥 All ages

  4. 7:00pm
    🥁

    Garba and dandiya

    DJ Kiran and the Eastgate Dhol Collective keep the circle turning for a full hour. Dandiya sticks are free to borrow at the stage; flat, soft-soled shoes are kindest to the plaza.

    📍 Stage lawn🎵 Live dhol

  5. 8:00pm
    🙏

    Lakshmi aarti and the wall of diyas

    Pandit Ramesh Shastri leads the evening aarti with bells and a thali for everyone to pass. Afterwards, light a diya on the wall and make a wish for the year ahead.

    📍 Diya wall♿ Seating for elders

  6. 8:30pm
    🎇

    Mithai hour and the sparkler corner

    Last orders on hot jalebi, a lantern walk round the plaza and a supervised sparkler corner for families. Fire marshals are on hand and grown-ups hold the lighter.

    📍 Food Lane and Sparkler corner

  7. 9:30pm
    🎆

    Fireworks finale

    Twenty minutes of fireworks from the south lawn, fired by licensed pyrotechnicians. Everyone stays behind the barriers and faces the stage. Ear defenders are free at the info tent.

    📍 Fireworks lawn🛡️ Safety zone

  8. 9:50pm
    🫶

    Closing circle: Shubh Diwali

    A thank-you from the volunteers, a last round of applause for the stallholders, and the final diyas carried out to light the way home. Gates close at 10 pm.

🎨 Contest, 5 pm to 6:30 pm

Colour the square

Thirty-six plots, ninety minutes, one theme: Lights of Home. Enter solo or with a team of up to four, pick your style, and we'll hold a plot (and a colour kit, if you like) with your name on it.

  • 🕓Check in 4:30 to 4:55 pm at the Rangoli desk on the Rangoli Ground.
  • 📏Plots are 4 by 4 ft for solo and pairs, 6 by 6 ft for teams of three or four.
  • 🌸Powder, petals, rice flour, lentils and small diyas are welcome. Chalk, glitter and paint are not: the plaza is washed down after.
  • ♿Step-free plots near Gate A, with table-top boards for artists who prefer not to kneel.

🖌️ Craft tent, 4:30 to 7 pm

Paint-a-diya workshop

Pick a plain clay diya, a brush and a palette of pink, green and gold, and decorate your own. Takes 40 minutes, dries while you wander, and it's yours to carry to the wall of diyas at the aarti. Free, for all ages; under-8s paint with a grown-up.

🥁 Stage lawn, 7 pm to 8 pm

Garba, dandiya and one very big circle

The circle starts small and grows until it wraps the whole stage lawn. You don't need to know a single step: the 6:30 warm-up covers the basics, and the person next to you will happily fix your clap.

  1. 1
    Do taali: two claps, step inClap twice, step to the right, bring your feet together.
  2. 2
    Teen taali: three claps, turnThree claps and a half-turn with arms wide. Eyes on the person ahead.
  3. 3
    Dandiya: sticks up, partner tapTap your partner's sticks at shoulder height, then swap sides on the beat.

🛡️

A fenced 200-foot launch zone

The south lawn is fenced from 9:00 pm. Marshals in orange vests close it, and nobody crosses until the all-clear. Never lift a barrier, even for a lost shoe.

👁️

Best views

Stand on the stage lawn or the north steps and face south. The food lane and the wall of diyas sit outside the fallout area.

🎧

Little ears, big bangs

Free ear defenders for children and anyone sensitive to noise at the info tent. The quiet zone on the library steps has a clear view and a softer sound.

🐕

Pets

Fireworks frighten animals, so please leave pets at home. If they must come, keep them leashed and use the quiet zone. Assistance dogs are always welcome.

🩹

First aid, all evening

Trained first-aiders and a medical tent beside the info tent, open from 3:30 pm until the last guest has left.

💨

Wind check at 9:00 pm

If gusts top 20 mph the show slips to 9:45 pm. We'll announce it from the stage and the info tent, and on the info line, 555-0142.

❓ Before you come

Good questions

🎟️Is it really free?▾

Yes. Entry, the rangoli contest, the diya-painting workshop and the shuttle from the station cost nothing. You only pay for food, drinks and whatever you buy from a stall.

🍲Is the food vegetarian? What about allergies?▾

Everything on Food Lane is vegetarian, and many dishes are vegan or Jain-friendly: use the filters in the stall directory. Every stall posts an allergen card. Sweets often contain nuts, so please ask before you buy for someone with an allergy.

☂️What if it rains?▾

The mela runs rain or shine. Food Lane and Craft Row sit under awnings, the stage has a roof, and ponchos are free at Gate A. If a thunderstorm warning is in force, the fireworks move to Sunday 8 November at 7 pm and we'll say so on the info line.

🎇Can I bring my own fireworks, sparklers or lanterns?▾

Please don't. The only fireworks are the licensed show at 9:30 pm, and the only sparklers are in the supervised corner at 8:30 pm. Sky lanterns and open-flame lanterns are not allowed on the plaza.

🐕Can I bring my dog?▾

Assistance dogs are always welcome. Pets are better at home on the night because of the fireworks; if yours must come, keep them leashed and use the quiet zone on the library steps.

👥We're a group of twenty. Can we all come?▾

Of course. Each RSVP takes up to 12 people, so send two for a bigger group. For temple, school or office outings of 30 or more, email the info line so we can mark a meeting spot at Gate A.

🚲Where do I leave my bike or pram?▾

There's a free staffed bike valet at Gate B and a buggy park beside the info tent. Prams and wheelchairs can use every path on the plaza.

🤝Can my business sponsor the mela?▾

Yes, and thank you. The Eastgate Indian Cultural Association is a volunteer-run non-profit. Local businesses can sponsor a rangoli plot, a lantern string or a slice of the fireworks fund. Write to mela@eastgate-ica.example.

🪔

See you in the square

Diwali is the story of light finding its way home. For one evening, Rangoli Square is that home: a little louder, a lot brighter, and open to everyone who walks in. Bring someone who has never been.
