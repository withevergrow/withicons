🪷 A note from the hosts

Come hungry, come bright, bring a diya

Namaste, dear friends and family. Every year our little house on Jasmine Row fills up a bit more than it should, and every year we wouldn't have it any other way. This Diwali we would love you to be at our table.

We begin with a short Lakshmi puja at 7:15, sit down to a long, slow, entirely vegetarian dinner, and finish in the courtyard with sparklers for the children (and the grown-ups who pretend they don't want one). The diyas will be lit on every windowsill by then.

Come in your brightest clothes, leave your shoes at the porch, and bring one diya of your own if you have it. Everything else is taken care of.

👩 👨
Anjali & Rohanwith Aarav, Meera and Dadi

🌙 Sunday 8 November

How the evening unfolds

From the first cup of chai to the last sparkler: four unhurried hours, in this order.

  1. 6:306:30 pm

    🚪 Arrival, tilak and chai

    The porch light is a string of four hundred marigold bulbs, so you can't miss us. Leave your shoes on the porch rack and Anjali or Rohan will put a tilak on your forehead. Masala chai and kesar-badam milk are waiting in the hall.

    ☕ Chai and kesar milk 🎨 Children go straight to the rangoli corner
  2. 7:157:15 pm

    🙏 Lakshmi puja

    Our family pandit, Shri Vishnu Joshi, leads a short puja in the front room: kalash, mantras, flowers and sweets as offerings, and a diya lit by every household. Sit on the floor or on the chairs along the wall. Phones on silent, please; photographs are welcome after the aarti.

    ⏳ About 45 minutes 🔔 Aarti around 7:55 🎁 Prasad for everyone
  3. 🍲8:15 pm

    🍽️ Dinner, served thali-style

    No buffet queue. Steel katoris come to the long table course by course, so nobody balances a plate on a knee. Seconds are not just allowed, they are expected. Everything is vegetarian, and the Jain and vegan dishes are marked on the thali card.

    🌿 Pure vegetarian 👪 One long table, 40 seats
  4. 🍥9:30 pm

    🍬 Mithai and chai

    Kaju katli, motichoor ladoo, mohanthal and hot jalebi with rabri come out on the brass stand, next to whatever sweets you bring. Fresh masala chai and copper tumblers of kesar-badam doodh go round. Everyone leaves with a small box for the road.

    🟠 Bring a sweet if you like
  5. 10:0010:00 pm

    ✨ Sparklers in the courtyard

    Phuljhadi for every age, one at a time, lit by a grown-up. The children go first, then the rest of us. Details below.

  6. 🌙10:30 pm

    🏠 Shubh ratri

    The last diya goes out at half past ten, rickshaws and cars are called, and we wave you off from the porch.

The muhurat, in plain language

🏺 When exactly is the puja?

Lakshmi puja is done in the early evening, after sunset. For our city this year our pandit-ji gives a window of 6:52 to 8:34 pm. We light the first diya at 7:15 and finish with aarti around 7:55.

Auspicious windowOur puja
6:307:158:159:3010:0010:30

Please be seated by 7:10. If you arrive a little late, slip in at the back; nobody minds, and you will still catch the aarti.

🏺 What happens in the puja

  1. 1
    Ganesh vandanaA short prayer to Ganesha, who clears the way for everything that follows.
  2. 2
    Kalash and the first diyaThe kalash is set down, and the oldest person in the room lights the first diya.
  3. 3
    Offerings to LakshmiFlowers, rice and sweets, while Pandit-ji chants. Repeat the words, or simply listen.
  4. 4
    Aarti and prasadEveryone stands, sings or claps along, and leaves the room with a tilak and a sweet.

✨ 10:00 pm

🎇 Sparklers, the gentle kind

Phuljhadi only: no bangers, no rockets. A sparkler burns for about a minute, so we hand them out in small rounds and everyone gets two.

  • 🛡️A grown-up lights and holds every child's sparkler. Under-fives watch from the porch with Dadi.
  • 💧Two buckets of water at the gate, and a hose within reach.
  • 🐕Pinto the dog hates loud noise, which is one more reason for sparklers only.

🎁 The mithai table

Bring a sweet, take a box home

💡 Good to know

Small things that make the night easy

The questions our friends always ask, answered before they ask them.

👘

Dress code

Festive Indian or smart. Silks, kurtas, anarkalis, sherwanis and saris are all welcome, and so is a good jacket. Jewel tones and gold glow beautifully by lamplight. Black is traditionally skipped on Lakshmi puja evening.

🚪

Shoes off

Shoes come off at the porch, where there is a rack and a bench. Socks are fine, and there is a basket of spare indoor chappals in sizes 5 to 11. The floors are cool, so bring socks if you are the sort who feels it.

🪔

Bring a diya

Bring one diya if you have it. We provide oil, wicks and matches, plus sixty spare clay ones the children painted last weekend. After the puja, yours goes on a windowsill for the night.

🅿️

Parking

Street parking on Jasmine Row is free after 6 pm. The overflow is St. Anselm's Hall lot, two minutes' walk (the sign says Visitors). Please keep the driveway at No. 297 clear.

👶

Little ones

Children are the guests of honour. There is a high chair, a quiet room upstairs with mats and cushions, and a kids' plate of plain rice, dal and paneer cubes. Tell us ages in the RSVP.

♿

Step-free access

Step-free entry through the side gate on Maple Lane, a ground-floor bathroom, and a chair in the puja room for anyone who prefers one. Tell us in the RSVP if you need anything else.

🎁

Gifts

No gifts, please: your company is the gift. If you must, a box of sweets for the mithai table, or a donation to the Jasmine Row food bank, which Rohan will collect in a little brass pot by the door.

📷

Photos & phones

Photographs are lovely after the aarti. During the puja we ask for phones on silent and in pockets. We will share a group album the morning after, so you can put the camera down.

    📖 Questions

    Before you ask

    If it isn't here, message us. Anjali answers faster than Rohan, but only slightly.

    💬 WhatsApp Anjali: 555-0178 📞 Call Rohan: 555-0142 ✉️ hello@mehtahouse.example
    Can I bring a friend, or my parents?▾

    Of course; just count them in your RSVP, adults and children separately. We have forty seats and the counter above shows how many are left, so reply early. If you are a group of more than five, send Anjali a message first.

    Is the food really all vegetarian?▾

    Yes. No meat, fish, egg or gelatine will cross the threshold that night. Dairy is used freely, and the vegan and Jain dishes are marked on the thali card. Tell us about any allergy in the RSVP.

    How long is the puja, and must I take part?▾

    About forty-five minutes. Sit and watch, or join in; both are welcome. Restless children can wander to the back with Dadi, who will have a plate of fruit waiting.

    What should the children wear?▾

    Anything bright and comfortable. The rangoli powder washes out, but it is enthusiastic, so we hand out aprons. Many children like to wear their festive outfit after the rangoli corner closes at 7:10.

    Will there be alcohol?▾

    No. There is masala chai, kesar-badam doodh, jaljeera and nimbu masala soda, all on the thali card, and nobody has ever complained.

    Can I bring flowers or a gift?▾

    A marigold garland for the front door is always welcome. Otherwise, a box of sweets for the mithai table is perfect. No other gifts, please.

    What about fireworks?▾

    Sparklers only, lit by grown-ups, with water at hand. No bangers or rockets: our neighbours, the babies and Pinto the dog all thank you.

    What if I need to cancel?▾

    Message Anjali as early as you can and your seat goes to someone on the waiting list. Life happens, and no explanation is needed.
